### Introduction & Key Objectives
The word 'Trigonometry' is derived from the Greek words 'Trigonon' (meaning a triangle) and 'Metron' (meaning to measure). Originally, trigonometry was restricted to studying the relationships between the sides and angles of a triangle. However, it has evolved into a foundational pillar of calculus, wave mechanics, sound analysis, and alternating current circuits. This chapter introduces generalized angles, dynamic measurement systems, and foundational trigonometric functions.

After studying this chapter, you will be able to:
* Define a sign-dependent generalized angle using rotational vectors.
* Understand the two primary systems of angle measurement: **Sexagesimal (Degree) system** and **Circular (Radian) system**.
* Convert measurements fluently between degrees and radians.
* Define trigonometric functions of a real number using a unit circle definition.
* Establish and apply fundamental trigonometric identities.
* Determine the exact signs, domains, and ranges of trigonometric functions across the four coordinate quadrants.

### Core Concepts & Formulas

#### 1. Concept of a Generalized Angle
In geometry, angles are bounded between 0 and 180 degrees. In trigonometry, an angle is generated by rotating a ray from an **initial position** to a **terminal position**.
* **Positive Angle:** Generated if the rotation is **counter-clockwise**.
* **Negative Angle:** Generated if the rotation is **clockwise**.

#### 2. Systems of Measuring Angles
1. **Sexagesimal System (Degree System):**
   * 1 right angle $= 90^\circ$ (degrees)
   * $1^\circ = 60'$ (minutes)
   * $1' = 60''$ (seconds)

2. **Circular System (Radian System):**
   * A **radian** is the angle subtended at the center of a circle by an arc whose length is equal to the radius of the circle. It is denoted by $1^c$ or simply $1$ rad.
   * Relation between arc length ($s$), radius ($r$), and central angle ($\theta$ in radians):
   > $$\theta = \frac{s}{r} \implies s = r\theta$$

#### 3. Relation Between Degrees and Radians
Since a complete circular rotation covers an arc length of $2\pi r$, the central angle is $2\pi$ radians, which equals $360^\circ$.
> $$\pi \text{ radians} = 180^\circ$$
> * $1 \text{ radian} = \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} \approx 57^\circ 16' 22''$
> * $1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180} \text{ radians} \approx 0.01746 \text{ rad}$

#### 4. Unit Circle Definition of Trigonometric Functions
Let a point $P(x, y)$ lie on a circle of radius $1$ centered at the origin. If the radius vector $OP$ makes an angle $\theta$ with the positive x-axis:
* $\cos\theta = x$
* $\sin\theta = y$
* $\tan\theta = \frac{y}{x} \quad (x \neq 0)$



#### 5. Fundamental Trigonometric Identities
> 1. $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$
> 2. $1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta \quad (\theta \neq \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi, n \in \mathbb{Z})$
> 3. $1 + \cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta \quad (\theta \neq n\pi, n \in \mathbb{Z})$

#### 6. Signs of Trigonometric Functions by Quadrant
The quadrant position of the terminal ray determines the positive or negative sign of the function value:
* **Quadrant I ($0$ to $\frac{\pi}{2}$):** **All** functions ($\sin, \cos, \tan, \cot, \sec, \csc$) are positive.
* **Quadrant II ($\frac{\pi}{2}$ to $\pi$):** Only **$\sin$** and **$\csc$** are positive. (Others are negative).
* **Quadrant III ($\pi$ to $\frac{3\pi}{2}$):** Only **$\tan$** and **$\cot$** are positive. (Others are negative).
* **Quadrant IV ($\frac{3\pi}{2}$ to $2\pi$):** Only **$\cos$** and **$\sec$** are positive. (Others are negative).
* *Mnemonic:* **A**ll **S**ilver **T**ea **C**ups (ASTC Rule).

#### 7. Domain and Range Summary Table
| Function | Domain | Range |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\sin x$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $[-1, 1]$ |
| $\cos x$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $[-1, 1]$ |
| $\tan x$ | $\mathbb{R} - \{(2n+1)\frac{\pi}{2} : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ | $\mathbb{R}$ |
| $\sec x$ | $\mathbb{R} - \{(2n+1)\frac{\pi}{2} : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ | $(-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty)$ |
| $\csc x$ | $\mathbb{R} - \{n\pi : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ | $(-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty)$ |

### Step-by-Step Examples

**Example 1:** Convert $40^\circ 20'$ into radian measure.  
**Solution:**
1. Express the minutes as a fraction of a degree:
$$20' = \left(\frac{20}{60}\right)^\circ = \left(\frac{1}{100}\right)^\circ \rightarrow \text{Wait, } \frac{20}{60} = \frac{1}{3}^\circ$$
$$40^\circ 20' = \left(40 + \frac{1}{3}\right)^\circ = \left(\frac{121}{3}\right)^\circ$$
2. Apply the conversion factor ($\frac{\pi}{180}$):
$$\text{Radian measure} = \frac{121}{3} \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{121\pi}{540} \text{ radians}$$

**Example 2:** A wheel makes 360 revolutions in one minute. Through how many radians does it turn in one second?  
**Solution:**
1. Find revolutions per second:
$$\text{Revolutions in } 60 \text{ seconds} = 360 \implies \text{Revolutions in } 1 \text{ second} = \frac{360}{60} = 6$$
2. Calculate the radian angle for one full rotation:
$$1 \text{ full revolution} = 2\pi \text{ radians}$$
3. Compute total radians in 1 second:
$$\text{Angle turned in } 1 \text{ second} = 6 \times 2\pi = 12\pi \text{ radians}$$

**Example 3:** If $\cos x = -\frac{3}{5}$ and $x$ lies in the third quadrant, find the values of $\sin x$ and $\tan x$.  
**Solution:**
1. Use the core identity to find the numerical magnitude of $\sin x$:
$$\sin^2 x = 1 - \cos^2 x = 1 - \left(-\frac{3}{5}\right)^2 = 1 - \frac{9}{25} = \frac{16}{25}$$
$$\sin x = \pm \frac{4}{5}$$
2. Apply quadrant sign constraints: Since $x$ is in the third quadrant (Quadrant III), sine must be negative.
$$\sin x = -\frac{4}{5}$$
3. Compute tangent using its quotient identity:
$$\tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \frac{-4/5}{-3/5} = \frac{4}{3}$$
*(Note that tangent is correctly positive in Quadrant III).*

### Terminal Exercises & Self-Check Questions

1. **Radian Conversion Workout:** Express $-\frac{5\pi}{3}$ radians in the Sexagesimal degree format.
   * *Answer Hint:* Multiply by $\frac{180}{\pi}$: $-\frac{5\pi}{3} \times \frac{180}{\pi} = -300^\circ$.

2. **Arc Length Problem:** Find the radius of a circle in which a central angle of $60^\circ$ intercepts an arc of length $37.4 \text{ cm}$ (Take $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$).
   * *Step-by-Step Solution:* 1. Convert angle to radians: $\theta = 60 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{3} \text{ rad}$.
     2. Rearrange formula $s = r\theta \implies r = \frac{s}{\theta}$.
     3. Substitute values: $r = \frac{37.4}{\pi/3} = \frac{37.4 \times 3 \times 7}{22} = 35.7 \text{ cm}$.

3. **Identity Verification:** Prove that $\sqrt{\frac{1 - \sin\theta}{1 + \sin\theta}} = \sec\theta - \tan\theta$ for acute angles.
   * *Proof Pathway:* Rationalize the denominator inside the root:
     $$\text{LHS} = \sqrt{\frac{(1-\sin\theta)(1-\sin\theta)}{(1+\sin\theta)(1-\sin\theta)}} = \sqrt{\frac{(1-\sin\theta)^2}{\cos^2\theta}} = \frac{1-\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} = \frac{1}{\cos\theta} - \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} = \sec\theta - \tan\theta = \text{RHS}$$
