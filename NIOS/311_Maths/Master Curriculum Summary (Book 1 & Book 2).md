# NIOS Senior Secondary Mathematics (311)
## Master Curriculum Summary (Book 1 & Book 2)

---

### Part 1: Foundations & Internal Assessment (Book 1)
**Evaluation Profile:** Evaluated heavily through Tutor Marked Assignments (TMA) | **Weightage:** 20% of Total Course Assessment

#### Module I: Sets, Relations and Functions (Chapters 1 to 5)
* **Core Philosophy:** Establishes the formal mathematical language used in modern structural thinking. It transitions from collections of objects to pairing relations, and maps them to trigonometric geometric coordinates.
* **Key Learning Objectives:**
  * Master set operations (Unions, Intersections, Complements) and verify algebraic rules using **Venn Diagrams**.
  * Differentiate between Cartesian mappings, Relations, and formal single-valued **Functions**.
  * Standardize radian and degree system angle measurements using the foundational **Unit Circle**.
  * Use compound angles, transformation formulas ($C-D$ formulas), and structural rules (**Sine & Cosine Rules**) to solve oblique triangles.

#### Module II: Sequences and Series (Chapters 6 & 7)
* **Core Philosophy:** Examines predictable algebraic numerical growth patterns and discrete summations.
* **Key Learning Objectives:**
  * Solve the general $n^{\text{th}}$ term and dynamic summation profiles for **Arithmetic Progressions (AP)** and **Geometric Progressions (GP)**.
  * Embed discrete Arithmetic Means (AM) and Geometric Means (GM), proving the core inequality profile: $\text{AM} \ge \text{GM}$.
  * Evaluate convergent **Infinite Geometric Series** and compute complex series expansions using standard natural number power summation rules ($\sum n, \sum n^2, \sum n^3$).

#### Module III: Algebra-I (Chapters 8 to 12)
* **Core Philosophy:** Expands the boundaries of polynomial structures, equations, and advanced combinatorics.
* **Key Learning Objectives:**
  * Introduce the imaginary element $i = \sqrt{-1}$ to build **Complex Numbers**, plotting them on the **Argand Plane**.
  * Extract complex roots of Quadratic Equations using the discriminant ($D < 0$) framework.
  * Solve single-variable linear inequalities and plot boundary solution sets on real number lines.
  * Apply the **Fundamental Principle of Counting** to solve Permutations (arrangements where order matters) and Combinations (selections where order is irrelevant).
  * Expand higher-order polynomials using the **Binomial Theorem**, locating general, middle, and independent terms.

#### Module IV: Co-ordinate Geometry (Chapters 13 to 16)
* **Core Philosophy:** Bridges algebra and geometry by tracking spatial paths, lines, and conic shapes using precise numerical coordinates.
* **Key Learning Objectives:**
  * Calculate point distances, section ratios, and triangle areas on a standard 2D Cartesian plane.
  * Establish equations of **Straight Lines** across multiple standard forms (Slope-intercept, Two-point, Normal forms).
  * Define and analyze geometric properties of circles and **Conic Sections** (Parabolas, Ellipses, and Hyperbolas) based on their standard equations.

#### Module V: Statistics and Probability (Chapters 17 to 19)
* **Core Philosophy:** Provides tools to measure data dispersion and quantify chance or uncertainty.
* **Key Learning Objectives:**
  * Calculate measures of dispersion, including **Mean Deviation** and **Standard Deviation/Variance**, for both ungrouped and grouped datasets.
  * Define random experiments, sample spaces, and compound events.
  * Master classical and axiomatic **Probability** calculations, addition/multiplication theorems, and conditional probability distributions.

### Part 2: Public Examination Core (Book 2)
**Evaluation Profile:** Evaluated via the Final Centralized Public Examination | **Weightage:** 80% of Total Course Assessment

#### Module VI: Algebra-II (Chapters 20 to 22)
* **Core Philosophy:** Introduces multi-dimensional array notation to store data linear structures and solve systems of equations.
* **Key Learning Objectives:**
  * Perform algebraic operations (addition, scalar multiplication, and row-column multiplication) on **Matrices**.
  * Evaluate the **Determinant** of square matrices and apply properties to simplify expressions.
  * Compute the **Inverse of a Matrix** ($A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|} \text{adj}(A)$) and use it to solve systems of linear equations via Matrix Methods or Cramer's Rule.

#### Module VII: Relations and Functions (Chapters 23 & 24)
* **Core Philosophy:** Refines functional properties to establish mapping constraints and reverse trigonometry.
* **Key Learning Objectives:**
  * Classify complex relations (Reflexive, Symmetric, Transitive) to identify **Equivalence Relations**.
  * Determine structural behaviors of functions, such as **One-to-One (Injective)** and **Onto (Surjective)** mappings.
  * Restrict principal domains to evaluate **Inverse Trigonometric Functions** and apply their algebraic properties.

#### Module VIII: Calculus (Chapters 25 to 32)
* **Core Philosophy:** The study of continuous change. It is split into differential calculus (tracking instantaneous rates of change) and integral calculus (accumulating continuous areas). **This is the highest-weightage module of the exam.**
* **Key Learning Objectives:**
  * Evaluate limits using factorization or L'Hôpital's Rule, and verify structural **Continuity**.
  * Differentiate algebraic, trigonometric, exponential, and logarithmic functions using Chain, Product, and Quotient rules.
  * Use derivatives to analyze real-world applications, such as finding rates of change, tangents, normals, and optimizing functions via **Maxima and Minima**.
  * Apply **Indefinite and Definite Integration** (substitution, parts, partial fractions) to compute areas under curves.
  * Formulate and solve first-order, first-degree **Differential Equations** using variable-separable methods.

#### Module IX: Vectors and Three Dimensional Geometry (Chapters 33 to 36)
* **Core Philosophy:** Extends coordinate geometry into 3D spatial environments using magnitude-and-direction vectors.
* **Key Learning Objectives:**
  * Represent positions in 3D coordinate space using distance and direction cosines.
  * Perform vector algebra, focusing on the geometric utility of **Dot Products** (scalar) and **Cross Products** (vector).
  * Derive vector and Cartesian equations for **Straight Lines** and **Planes** in 3D space, calculating angles and shortest distances between them.

#### Module X: Linear Programming & Mathematical Reasoning (Chapters 37 & 38)
* **Core Philosophy:** Combines optimization modeling with formal logical syntax.
* **Key Learning Objectives:**
  * Formulate **Linear Programming Problems (LPP)** based on real-world resource constraints.
  * Graphically solve bounded and unbounded feasible regions to find optimal maximum or minimum values.
  * Analyze compound mathematical statements using logical connectives (AND, OR, Implications) and construct rigorous truth tables.
